# Data quality and transparency (E5)

Answers the OPEN item in master_plan section 8 (20% of the score). Numbers in double square brackets are filled by `fill_numbers.py` from `demo/data/demo_bundle.json` (run of 2026-11-14); the filled copy is in `filled/`.

## 1. The data is synthetic, and we say so

No real patient, hospital or decision data is used anywhere. Each simulated patient is drawn from a hidden condition (cancer, a commonly confused non-cancer condition, or nothing serious), and each test result is drawn from that condition using the test's sensitivity and specificity (`params/test_accuracy.yaml`, each row with its source). Cancer is kept rare so that aggressive pathways are not rewarded for free. The demo, video and deck carry the label "synthetic data" on every results screen.

Why this is acceptable: the brief asks for a working system; real Finnish pathway data is not obtainable in a weekend. What we offer in its place is traceability and a check against a published pathway, and a design that runs unchanged on real logs.

## 2. Every parameter is traceable

- Each number in a scenario file carries a `source:` key pointing to an entry in `params/sources.yaml`, which records publication, page or table, and the value as printed.
- Numbers with no published source are labelled `source: assumption`. Counted from the scenario files the engine reads (every `source:` tag, read by `params/ledger.py`; calibration targets and repeated decision mixes are not counted as inputs): the Finnish scenario has 49 input blocks (`docs/ledger.md`), 9 sourced (5 if blocks whose own note mentions an assumption or calibration are left out) and 40 assumptions; the Norwegian scenario has 25 input blocks, 22 sourced and 3 assumptions (more Norwegian parameters are calibrated, see section 3). The Finnish assumptions cover every server capacity, every booking lead and urgent booking lead, the GP, nurse and specialist decision probabilities and most prevalences.
- The demo's "Sources and assumptions" screen and `docs/parameters.md` list a wider table (131 rows, 113 sourced, 18 assumptions). Read it as evidence and calibration targets (published intervals, survival, incidence counts), not as the count of simulator inputs that are sourced.
- A judge can click from any result to the parameters behind it.

## 3. Calibration against Stokstad 2019

The check is described in `docs/validation.md`. We fit only the parameters the paper does not report, on the observed pathway only; the other two flowcharts are predictions. Fitted: 11 of 192 points of the first grid reproduce the observed pathway (share 40% plus or minus 4 points, median 46.5 days). The best fit of that grid is the setting behind the single numbers below.

| Flowchart | Published (Stokstad 2019) | Our simulation (held-out) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Observed pathway | 40% within 42 d | 40% (fitted) | fitted to this number |
| Faster PET-CT only (7 d) | 48% (not significant) | 60% at the best fit | predicted, not fitted: we over-predict |
| Authors' reordered pathway | 80% | 80% at the best fit | predicted, not fitted: consistent, not exact |

**The prediction is a band, not a point.** The first grid was narrow and its accepted points sat on its edge. The robustness check (`docs/validation.md`, "Robustness"; `python scripts/norway_robustness.py`) extended the grid to 1920 points. 184 settings now fit the observed pathway equally well, and for them the authors' pathway is predicted at 62 to 86% (typically about 75%; middle 80% of settings: 66 to 82%). 64% of those settings fall inside the published 95% interval (72-87%). The first grid's range (78 to 86%) was the top corner of this band and is not used as a headline. Why the band is wide: the two fitted targets (share and median) carry almost the same information, so they cannot pin down five inputs the paper does not report; the band is 24 points wide. The single best-fit figure (80%) is therefore consistent with the paper, not a sharp out-of-sample hit, and we do not say we predicted it exactly.

**What holds in every setting.** The ranking: the authors' reordered pathway beats the faster scanner alone, which beats today, in all 184 of 184 settings (and at 1028 of 1043 predicted grid points). The reordering adds 24 to 47 points over today (the paper's model estimate: 40 points). The size of each gain is uncertain by about plus or minus 12 points. Sampling procedures: simulated ratio optimal to observed 0.82 against 92/112 = 0.82 published (checked on the first grid only).

**Where it does not match.** The faster-PET-only result is over-predicted: 60% at the best fit against the published 48% (interval 39-58%), about 12 points; across the 184 settings 50 to 74% (median 60.5%), and only 34% of settings fall inside the published interval. We tested two explanations (a re-discussion at the tumour board when work-up is incomplete, and delays that are correlated within a patient). Neither fixed it. The model puts PET-CT on the critical path of most patients, and the paper's per-step medians do not show that. So the model is likely to overstate gains from speeding up a single step; that makes the order-versus-capacity conclusion conservative, but it is an explanation we have not confirmed.

**Caveat on "predicted, not fitted".** The calibrated observed practice (PET-CT ordered when the referral is triaged) is carried into the authors' pathway. With PET-CT ordered at triage for 100%, 50% or 0% of patients, the best-fit authors' pathway gives 80%, 73% or 66% (the last is PET-CT ordered at the first visit, as the paper describes). We keep the word "predicted" but not as an independent validation of the 80%.

**What was assumed.** Two structural overlaps the paper does not report (decision D16), plus the test results of individual patients. The paper's 80% is itself a model estimate from re-timing real patients, so we compare shares, not individuals. All of the paper's patients already had cancer, so the check covers specialist care, not primary-care filtering. **Structure check.** Our version of the authors' pathway differs from the paper's text in two ways that offset each other: treatment waits for the tissue sample (removing that wait raises ours by about 13.5 points) and PET-CT is ordered at triage instead of at the first visit (raises ours by about 14.1). A stricter reading of the paper's text (PET-CT at the first visit, treatment not waiting for sampling) gives a median of 79% over the 184 settings (64 to 90%; 139 of 184 inside the published 72-87%) and 77% at the best fit. Caveats: we read summaries of the paper, not its figures, and the observed pathway was not recalibrated under that reading; the shipped scenario is unchanged and this is an open decision for the human lead. We read text summaries of the paper, not its figures. It is one hospital and 100 patients; it supports "reordering beats single-step speed-ups in this setting", not a forecast of real shares within 42 days.

## 4. Proposal from revealed information, acceptance by simulated truth

- **Proposing.** The search proposes a change using only what steps revealed in the run log (results, waits, queues), never the hidden condition (a test scrambles the hidden fields after a run and gets identical proposals). It reasons over groups of patients, because judging a test patient by patient after the fact is biased (later tests are run on patients who failed earlier ones).
- **The menu.** The search chooses among about 69 single changes in the Norwegian scenario (the Finnish search evaluated 21). The Norwegian menu included each of the authors' changes as one edit, and the list of clinically needed steps was hand-written; the search was not told which edits the authors made, but so "blind" means blind to the hidden condition, not blind to the answer: it is a search over a menu we wrote, and it is not a discovery from nothing. Starting from the observed pathway and without being told the answer, the optimiser arrived at the authors' redesign (their three changes, plus one overlapping change that needs a clinical check) and performs about the same as the authors' pathway built without a wait for sampling (88.3% against 88.2% at the best fit; within 0.2 to 2.5 points in every setting). It did not beat the authors. Without the fourth change the optimiser still gives 85% at the best fit with the scanner and 65% without; the fourth change is worth 2 to 4 points and overlaps with "tumour board before sampling". It was not in the paper and needs a clinical check. The optimiser's apparent lead over our simulated authors' pathway (88% against 80%) is not a finding about care: our version of the authors' pathway makes treatment wait for a tissue sample that starts after the tumour board, while the paper takes the sample while the patient waits for treatment; with that wait removed the authors' pathway gives 88%. The optimiser's flowcharts were found once, at the best-fit setting, and then evaluated unchanged on all 184 settings that fit the observed pathway (`docs/validation.md`, "Optimiser robustness, ablation and structure check"): with the faster scanner 88% at the best fit; 78 to 93% across all 184 settings (median 88%); with no new equipment 67% at the best fit; 48 to 77% across all 184 settings (median 65%), against 36 to 44% for today's pathway. Without new equipment it beats today's pathway in 184 of 184 settings but beats the faster scanner alone in only 128 of 184; with the scanner it beats the scanner alone in 184 of 184.
- **Accepting.** A proposed change is kept only if a rerun of the same patients (common random numbers) shortens time to treatment without more missed cancers and within the other guardrails. That rerun uses the simulated truth, which in real life nobody has. In real life the acceptance step is a pilot.
- **Consequence.** Our claim is "this change is worth piloting", not "this change works in Finland".
- **Overfitting.** Best-of-many on the same patients selects on noise (decision D6). We accept on a training cohort and report on held-out cohorts the search never saw. The gap between training and held-out is shown below.

## 5. Held-out reporting

| Run | Training cohort, after change | Held-out, after change | Selection optimism (training gain minus held-out gain, pp) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Norwegian, search with the faster scanner | 88% | 88% | 1.7 |
| Norwegian, search without new equipment | 66% | 67% | -0.6 |
| Finnish synthetic county, target 90 d | 29% | 33% | -0.2 |

Held-out cohorts hold about 1 000 simulated patients (Norwegian) and about 16 177 (Finnish) each. Headline figures in the video and deck are the held-out numbers. The optimism column is as reported by the optimiser (it compares the predicted with the rerun result); a negative value means the held-out result was higher than the training one.

Finnish guardrails on the held-out cohort (suggested change against baseline; the tissue-confirmed guardrail is active because bronchoscopy and needle biopsy are marked as confirming): missed cancers 27.5 to 27.5 per cohort, tests per patient 0.41 to 0.41, median days for non-cancer patients to leave the pathway 16.8 to 16.8. The headline is the mean of two held-out cohorts. On 12 independent cohorts of the same size (`docs/ranges.md`) today's share ranges from 14% to 25% and the suggested change's from 24% to 42%; the gain is 9 to 19 points (mean 15) and positive in 12 of 12 cohorts. Sensitivity runs on the accepted flowchart itself (`docs/ranges.md`): the gain stays positive when each of 26 assumptions is varied one at a time (smallest 0 points, when CT capacity is one slot lower) and in 100% of 16 joint draws (8 to 21 points, 5th to 95th percentile). The older sensitivity runs (sensitivity_external_rapid_ct.json, sensitivity_external_v4.json, sensitivity_finland_equity.json, sensitivity_finland_lung.json, sensitivity_finland_lung_capacity_control.json, sensitivity_stokstad.json, `docs/sensitivity.md`) cover the hand-written order-together pathway.

## 6. Known limitations

- Synthetic data; capacities for Finland are assumptions fitted to published waiting ranges.
- One region, one disease (lung cancer), one flowchart family; no differences between counties.
- One validation study, from one Norwegian centre with 100 patients; its 80% is a model estimate. Our prediction of it is a band (62 to 86%), not a point.
- Targets: the Norwegian check uses the study's 42 days. The Finnish scenario clock starts at first contact, where every flowchart is at 0% within 42 days, so the Finnish headline uses a **provisional 90-day target (decision D20, team call pending)**. The Finnish statutory limits are 21 days to assessment and 180 days to treatment ([IHE](https://ihe.se/app/uploads/2026/04/IHE-Cancer-Dashboard-Finland_.pdf)); we found no Finnish first-contact-to-treatment target. We do not claim to match the official Finnish monitor.
- The Norwegian calibration fits two overlaps that the paper does not report (D16), and its two targets act as one constraint; the faster-PET-only prediction misses by about 12 points at the best fit.
- The Finnish held-out result rests on two held-out cohorts and a single optimiser run. The older sensitivity analysis covers the hand-written order-together comparison; the accepted reorders are covered in `docs/ranges.md` (one seed per variant, 26 shortlisted assumptions). The urgent-booking finding (18% / 44% / 61% at 0 / 50 / 100% of the assumed lead reduction) is conditional on assumed booking leads and is not part of the headline.
- Guardrails: the Norwegian missed-cancer check is vacuous (every patient has cancer, no discharge exit). GP-first was blocked on the training patients by half a missed cancer per cohort, which is noise-level; on unseen patients it looked neutral to positive, so it needs a paired test.
- Decisions in the simulator runs shown are made by rules and placeholder decision tables (D4, D11, D51). Our own small decision-model benchmark is separate; in the simulator loop it matched the hand-written rule, and showed that per-patient choices overload a shared queue unless queue state is visible. The team's fine-tuned decision model (JEV) is connected through an adapter and a step-through tool (`docs/platform/jev-birch.md`); its scores are relative to the offered options, not disease probabilities, and no headline number depends on it.
- No simulated doctors: the starting flowchart is hand-written and the policy is rule-based or a placeholder table (decisions D4, D11, D51).
- Equity groups are modelled assumptions (D9); see the equity note for what is and is not sourced.
- Several sources could not be read in full here: Stokstad full text, Khalife 2025, Toronto and radiotherapy-review papers, the Finnish Cancer Societies report. Where we relied on an abstract or a news summary the source list says so.
- Safety: a clinician approves every proposal; a reserved treatment slot while tests finish does not mean treatment starts before diagnosis.

## 7. The synthetic patient engine and the primary-care loop

- **What exists.** A catalogue (`catalogue/lung-primary-care`, locked as `catalogue-v1`) of 11 conditions, 42 hidden variables and 26 tests, with accuracy, turnaround and cost for each test. 279 of its 392 sourced-or-assumed entries are tagged assumption and 113 cite a source (`catalogue/lung-primary-care`, counted as `demo/export_start.py` counts). **No clinician has read it; it is a draft.**
- **Cards.** Each card holds a hidden truth and the pre-written result of every test, for every state and attempt (misses and false alarms drawn from the catalogue's accuracy). Today: 500 template-written cards (`libraries/ref500`, id L_8a99f4d9d9fa9c5f, audit passes 40 of 40 checks) and 35 model-written cards in 2 libraries. **Known problem, found by the independent review (`docs/review_w7.md`, B1) and fixed in the code:** in cards written with the old result prompt (g2@1) the wording of test reports gave away which results are false alarms (50% of false-alarm positive reports hedge, against 2% of true positives). The audit did not catch it; the review did. The fix has three parts: with the new prompt (g2@2) the engine sets every value and the language model writes report text from the reported values only, never seeing the truth; reports of old cards are rebuilt from values before any decider or training row sees them; and the audit has a new check on report wording. Status of the new prompt: the new result prompt (g2@2: the engine sets every value, the language model writes report text from the reported values only) has not yet been run on the real model; the 35 model-written cards in the repository use the old prompt g2@1, and their report text is rebuilt from values before any decider sees it. The mix is stratified, not realistic prevalence. All of it is synthetic.
- **The information boundary.** Decisions see only the visible record: background, presenting symptoms, and the results of tests already done. Hidden conditions, hidden findings, the planned error of each test and the card's id are never sent to a decider; a patient handle reveals a result only when that test is ordered. The record has two layers: a mechanical log written by the engine, and assessments written by the decider (D49). The training export keeps labels outside the input.
- **The primary-care loop** uses placeholder decision tables, and every behaviour number is an assumption. it reproduces the calibrated chain at the edge of the tolerances: 8 of 8 checks on the seeds it was tuned on, 6 of 8 on fresh seeds, 7 of 8 pooled over eight seeds (`docs/encounter_results.md`); the one pooled miss is non-cancer patients referred (11.0% against the chain's 8.0%, tolerance 3 points), and the tuning was done on the first four seeds, so the in-sample result is the optimistic one. Anything shown from it is a mechanism, not a Finnish estimate. The numbers it gives: a cancer waits about 25 days to referral (the chain: 27); about one cancer in ten (9%, 4 to 13% over eight seeds) is first treated as something else and those wait about 4 times as long (median about 100 days against 24, wide interval). No difference between age, sex, smoking or region groups is established.
- **Backgrounds** now come from a population model (`populations/lung_primary_care.yaml`, D53: presenters' ages, smoking by age and sex, pack-years from age and smoking history, an age relative risk per condition) after the review found they were drawn without one. Most of its numbers are assumptions and are labelled so in the file. The catalogue hash is unchanged; library ids changed.
- **What we do not know.** Whether a model trained on these cards behaves sensibly on real patients. Template wording may be learnable; real records are messier; the catalogue's numbers are not clinically reviewed. The planned test is to train on template cards and test on model-written cards (`docs/synthetic_data_for_jev.md`); that shows robustness to wording, not validity on real patients.
