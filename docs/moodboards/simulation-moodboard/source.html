The colour rule

Amber is waiting. Mint is the good outcome. Cyan is our change. Red is the one we must not hide. A viewer should read the story with the sound off.

The balance

About 95 percent black. Colour only on dots, piles and a few numerals. No panels, no gradients, no illustration, nothing decorative behind the dots.

03 · Motion grammar

How the dots behave

Dots
  • Every dot is a simulated patient, same cohort in every view
  • They travel left to right through four stages, or climb a flowchart bottom to top
  • Treated in time: they land on the right as a mint block
Piles
  • A step that is slow collects dots into a pile above it
  • Piles grow while time runs and turn amber the longer patients wait
  • The biggest pile is the finding
The drop
  • One keypress: the change is applied to the same patients on the same day
  • The piles dissolve and the dots flow through, a cyan fast lane appears
  • The mint block jumps up, the number rises

Lean into
  • Black stage, round dots, nothing else competing
  • Time shown as distance and pile height
  • One number per moment, big
  • Today on one side, the change on the other, same patients
  • Sparse monospaced labels, dry and precise
  • Amber glow only where someone waits
Steer away from
  • Faces, hospitals, stock imagery, smiling doctors
  • Bright backgrounds, gradients, glossy 3D, drop shadows
  • Old computers, CRT monitors, terminal windows
  • Rainbow palettes: only the meaning colours plus the neutrals
  • Anything that blames clinicians. The subject is the queue
  • Stylised "AI" imagery, neural-net clichés, glowing brains
