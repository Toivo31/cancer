## The problem in Finland

### `fi-survival-gap` · Published research

Five-year relative survival in lung cancer for men is about 17% in Finland against 27 to 30% in Denmark, Norway and Sweden.

- **Source:** IHE2026 (2026), registry (via a dashboard report); figure: 17% vs 27-30% (men, NORDCAN 2019-2023); we read: **full_text**. <https://ihe.se/app/uploads/2026/04/IHE-Cancer-Dashboard-Finland_.pdf>
- **Strength:** Consistent with the Finnish Cancer Registry (men 16.2% for 2019-2023, docs/evidence_research.md section 3; that report is not in the source registry). We do not claim the waits cause the survival gap: for lung cancer the delay-to-mortality evidence is null on average (see 'What we could not support').
- **Shown on:** docs/pitch/deck.md: slide 2; docs/pitch/numbers_sheet.md: D2; demo/start.html: The problem

### `fi-gp-appointment-14d` · Published research

A health-centre appointment takes about 14 days in Finland, against 2 to 7 days elsewhere in the Nordics.

- **Source:** KHALIFE2025 (2025), survey (42 specialists plus two workshops; not registry data); figure: 14 d vs 2-7 d (typical wait, survey mode); we read: **full_text**. <https://doi.org/10.2340/1651-226X.2025.42778>
- **Strength:** One survey source; THL primary-care data (35% wait over 14 d, cited in KHALIFE2025) point the same way. The numbers sheet (D3) still says the paper was 'not read in full', while params/sources.yaml records full text via a repository copy: the registry is taken as current.
- **Shown on:** docs/master_plan.md: Finland is slower at most steps; docs/pitch/deck.md: slide 2; demo/start.html: The problem

### `fi-specialist-14-28d` · Published research

The specialist consultation comes 14 to 28 days after the GP referral in Finland, against 7 to 14 days elsewhere in the Nordics.

- **Source:** KHALIFE2025 (2025), survey; figure: 14-28 d vs 7-14 d; we read: **full_text**. <https://doi.org/10.2340/1651-226X.2025.42778>
- **Strength:** Survey modes, not measured waits. The Satakunta thesis (MATTILA2025) also puts most of the delay after the referral.
- **Shown on:** docs/master_plan.md: Finland is slower at most steps

### `fi-diagnosis-after-specialist` · Published research

- **Shown on:** docs/master_plan.md: Finland is slower at most steps

## Waits per step (what the simulator is given)

### `in-fi-waits-half-published` · Our simulation

In the Finnish baseline, 48% of the waiting days on a treated cancer's critical path sit at steps whose wait was set to match a published figure (GP appointment, KHALIFE2025; specialist visit, KHALIFE2025; treatment booking, IHE2026 all cancers; referral handling, PIRHA_PATH), and 52% at steps whose wait rests on our assumptions (CT, bronchoscopy, needle biopsy, PET-CT, weekly tumour board, chest X-ray, nurse visit, referral letter, the interval before a patient re-presents).

- **Run:** scenarios/finland_lung.yaml, flowchart baseline, seed 7, 18,800 patients, treated cancers after the warm-up, critical path (demo/research_split.py)
- **Inputs that drive it:** per-step booking leads and capacities (resources:), extra waits (tests:); which of them are published or assumed is listed in the split table
- **Strength:** This is an accounting of our own inputs, not evidence. The published-matched waits are survey modes (KHALIFE2025) or an all-cancer registry figure (IHE2026, see fi-diagnosis-to-treatment-37d); none is lung-specific measured data.
- **Shown on:** demo/index.html: screen 2 headline; docs/pitch/deck.md: slide 3

### `in-no-waits-published` · Our simulation

In the Norwegian model 83% of the waiting days are per-step waits taken from the published study (STOKSTAD2019); the rest are extra waits we calibrated so the observed pathway fits, and steps the paper did not report.

- **Run:** scenarios/stokstad.yaml, flowchart observed, seed 7, 2,000 patients (demo/research_split.py)
- **Inputs that drive it:** per-test `wait` distributions (STOKSTAD2019), `extra_wait` (assumption, calibrated), weekly board (STOKSTAD2019; weekday assumed)
- **Strength:** One hospital, 100 patients, waits reconstructed from medians and ranges in the paper.
- **Shown on:** demo/index.html: screen 2 for the Norwegian scenario; demo/flow.js: Where time is lost card

### `in-cxr-misses-one-in-five` · Published research

A chest X-ray misses about one in five lung cancers in symptomatic patients (sensitivity about 75%).

- **Source:** BRADLEY2021 (2021), single-centre prospective cohort (Leeds); figure: sensitivity 0.754 (86/114); we read: **full_text**. <https://bjgp.org/content/71/705/e280>
- **Strength:** Supported by a systematic review (BRADLEY2019, 77-80%, abstract) and the HSSIB investigation (HSSIB2021, which cites Bradley 2019). English data.
- **Shown on:** scenarios/finland_lung.yaml: chest_xray; demo/flow.js: exits (discharged cancers)

### `as-scanner-capacity` · Our assumption

The slot capacities and booking leads for CT, bronchoscopy, needle biopsy, PET-CT, chest X-ray and the weekly tumour board in the Finnish county are our assumptions.

- **Where:** scenarios/finland_lung.yaml: resources (ct_scanner, bronchoscopy, ttnb_slot, pet_scanner, xray, tumour_board)
- **Why:** We found no Finnish waits for PET-CT, bronchoscopy or needle biopsy, and no capacity data. The values are slices of county capacity chosen so the scarce tests run at 85-95% utilisation; the board is weekly as in Norway (STOKSTAD2019).
- **Strength:** Placeholders. Ask a Finnish clinician or the county for real numbers.
- **Shown on:** demo/index.html: screen 4; docs/pitch/deck.md: slide 10

## What causes delay

### `sim-fi-time-is-waiting` · Our simulation

In our synthetic Finnish county, 87% of the days to treatment are waiting (booking time and queues), not time spent in the tests and visits themselves.

- **Run:** demo/data/demo_bundle.json, finland_lung baseline stage breakdown (the number on demo/index.html screen 2)
- **Inputs that drive it:** per-step waits are inputs: about half matched to published figures, half assumed (in-fi-waits-half-published); service and report times are mostly assumptions with some published report times (PIRHA_PATH, STOKSTAD2019)
- **Strength:** The share follows from the inputs, it is not discovered by the model: if the waits are set from survey modes, most of the time is waiting by construction. Independent sources agree that waits dominate (KHALIFE2025, STOKSTAD2019), but none measures this share for Finland.

### `sim-booking-not-queue` · Our simulation

In the Finnish baseline most of the waiting is the fixed booking lead (57 days per treated cancer) rather than queueing for scarce capacity (9 days); the rest is extra waits we assumed (14 days), paperwork and the weekly board.

- **Run:** same run as in-fi-waits-half-published
- **Inputs that drive it:** booking leads (min_lead_days) tuned to published waits; capacities assumed
- **Strength:** A property of how waits were entered, not an observation: the model reproduces published waits mainly through booking leads. Real queues may be larger; no Finnish data separates the two.

In our model the stage with the most waiting is detection (30 days per treated cancer; detection is the primary-care part before a referral); the largest single waits are waiting for treatment slot (16 d), re-presents to gp (15 d), specialist visit (13 d).

## What the Norwegian study found

### `pub-stokstad-observed-40` · Published research

In the Norwegian hospital study, 40% of early-stage lung cancer patients started treatment within 42 days of referral.

- **Source:** STOKSTAD2019 (2019), single-hospital study; figure: 40% (95% CI 31-50), 100 patients; we read: **full_text**. <https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-019-4517-z>
- **Strength:** One centre, stage I-II only, 100 patients.
- **Shown on:** demo/index.html: screen 5; demo/flow.js: Check against the published study; docs/pitch/deck.md: slides 4 and 6

### `pub-stokstad-faster-pet-48` · Published research

Re-timing the same patients with a faster PET-CT (7 days) raised the share to 48%, a gain that was not statistically significant.

- **Source:** STOKSTAD2019 (2019), single-hospital study (authors' re-timing of observed patients); figure: 48% (95% CI 39-58); we read: **full_text**. <https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-019-4517-z>
- **Strength:** One centre; the re-timing is the authors' calculation, not an observed after-period.
- **Shown on:** demo/index.html: screen 5; demo/flow.js: validation card; docs/master_plan.md: Capacity versus order

### `pub-stokstad-reordered-80` · Published research

The authors estimated that reordering the steps (with the faster PET-CT) would bring 80% within 42 days; this is their model estimate, not an observed result.

- **Source:** STOKSTAD2019 (2019), single-hospital study (authors' model); figure: 80% (95% CI 72-87), model estimate; we read: **full_text**. <https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-019-4517-z>
- **Strength:** Never tested in practice in the paper. Pathway redesigns elsewhere did shorten times (see the unregistered sources below), which supports the direction.
- **Shown on:** demo/index.html: screen 5; demo/start.js: Why we trust the engine; docs/pitch/deck.md: slide 4

### `pub-stokstad-pet-wait` · Published research

During the study the median time from PET-CT referral to report was 20.5 days; a new scanner later cut it to 7 days or less.

- **Source:** STOKSTAD2019 (2019), single-hospital study; figure: 20.5 d (7-49); we read: **full_text**. <https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-019-4517-z>
- **Strength:** One centre.
- **Shown on:** docs/master_plan.md: Capacity versus order; scenarios/stokstad.yaml: pet_ct

Fitted only to the observed order, our model gives 40% treated within 42 days (the paper reports 40%). This is a fit, not a test.

- **Run:** docs/validation_results.json, demo/data/demo_bundle.json (best-fitting setting)
- **Inputs that drive it:** per-step waits from STOKSTAD2019; five unreported inputs calibrated (assumption)
- **Strength:** Agreement here is by construction.
- **Shown on:** demo/flow.js: validation card; demo/start.js: Why we trust the engine; docs/pitch/numbers_sheet.md: A1

### `sim-no-authors-band` · Our simulation

Predicted, not fitted: across all 184 settings that fit the observed pathway, the authors' reordered pathway comes out at 62 to 86%, typically about 75%; 64% of the settings fall inside the paper's interval.

- **Run:** docs/review_checks/norway_grid_extension.json, norway_grid_table.json (scripts/norway_robustness.py)
- **Inputs that drive it:** STOKSTAD2019 waits; the band comes from the five unreported inputs the data cannot pin down

### `sim-no-ranking-holds` · Our simulation

In all 184 fitting settings the authors' reordering beats the faster scanner alone, which beats today's pathway.

- **Run:** docs/review_checks/norway_ranking.json
- **Inputs that drive it:** as sim-no-authors-band
- **Strength:** The same ranking as the paper's own numbers (40 < 48 < 80), so it is a consistency check, not new evidence.

### `sim-no-faster-pet-overpredicted` · Our simulation

## Capacity versus order

In the Norwegian study a faster scanner alone barely helped, while reordering the steps (an estimate) roughly doubled the share treated on time.

- **Source:** STOKSTAD2019 (2019), single-hospital study; figure: 40% -> 48% (n.s.) vs 40% -> 80% (model estimate); we read: **full_text**. <https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-019-4517-z>

Supported in direction by redesign studies elsewhere (Royal Free, Toronto, a Welsh simulation, an English CXR-to-CT study) that are cited in docs/master_plan.md but not in the source registry. Limits: some tests are genuinely undersized (a London study), and fast tracks can crowd out other patients (Sweden).
- **Shown on:** docs/master_plan.md: Capacity versus order; demo/index.html: screen 4 lede; docs/pitch/deck.md: slide 4

- **Strength:** Consistent in direction with STOKSTAD2019; sizes rest on assumed capacities.
- **Shown on:** demo/index.html: screen 4; docs/pitch/deck.md: slide 4

### `sim-fi-one-more-slot` · Our simulation

## Data and AI limits

### `as-synthetic-patients` · Our assumption

All patients are synthetic; no real patient data is used anywhere.

- **Where:** patient_engine/, pathway_sim/patients.py, libraries/
- **Why:** Hackathon data rules and privacy; the brief allows synthetic data.
- **Strength:** A design choice. No claim of validity on real patients.
- **Shown on:** demo/start.html: Synthetic patients only; every page

### `as-primary-care-behaviour` · Our assumption

The primary-care loop uses placeholder decision tables; with them it reproduces the calibrated chain only at the edge of the tolerances (8 of 8 checks on its tuning seeds, 6 of 8 on fresh seeds).

- **Where:** catalogue/lung-primary-care/*.yaml, scenarios/finland_encounter*.yaml
- **Why:** No Finnish data on GP and nurse decisions; behaviour numbers are tuned to plausible practice.
- **Strength:** Placeholder; not measured in Finland.
- **Shown on:** demo/start.js: consistency line; docs/pitch/numbers_sheet.md: E4

### `as-catalogue-draft` · Our assumption

The care catalogue is a draft no clinician has read; 279 of its 392 entries are tagged assumption.

- **Where:** catalogue/lung-primary-care/*.yaml
- **Why:** Built overnight from literature where found; the rest labelled.
- **Strength:** Draft.
- **Shown on:** demo/start.js: Care catalogue; docs/pitch/numbers_sheet.md: E3

### `as-jev-not-in-headlines` · Our assumption

No headline number depends on the team's decision model (JEV); the headline runs use placeholder tables and rules.

- **Where:** jev/birch.py adapter; scenarios/*.yaml policies
- **Why:** The model is connected through an adapter for the demo, but it was not validated on a pathway task in time.
- **Strength:** Design choice.
- **Shown on:** docs/pitch/numbers_sheet.md: F3; docs/pitch/deck.md: slide 10

### `sim-dm-benchmark` · Our simulation

On a task where the right answer depends on the set of options, our own small decision model scores 84.5% against 79.0% for a parameter-matched dot-product baseline.

- **Run:** decision_model/README.md benchmark tables
- **Inputs that drive it:** a synthetic benchmark we designed (assumption)
- **Strength:** Our own benchmark; part of the gain is model capacity (control task). Not the team's JEV.
- **Shown on:** docs/pitch/deck.md: backup slides; docs/pitch/numbers_sheet.md: F1

### `sim-per-patient-optimisation-overloads` · Our simulation

A decision model that optimises each patient separately sends most patients to the shortest-looking queue and overloads it, unless it can see queue state.

- **Run:** decision model in the simulator loop (docs/master_plan.md section 10)
- **Inputs that drive it:** Finnish scenario capacities (assumption)
- **Strength:** Plausible and consistent with crowd-out concerns about fast tracks (Swedish interviews, not in the registry); shown only in our model.
- **Shown on:** docs/master_plan.md: Build status; docs/pitch/deck.md: slide 10

Basis: *published figure* = the input carries a registry source and its wait was set from it; *tuned to a published figure* = tagged assumption but its booking lead was calibrated to hit a published wait; *assumption* = neither. Extra waits are assumptions.

## What we could not support

Claims made in our documents that no source in `params/sources.yaml` backs, and inputs we could not source. Do not repeat these as sourced. Weak entries above are marked in their strength line (single survey, secondary, all-cancer instead of lung-specific, one hospital).

- **`delay-mortality-lung`: Faster care saves lives in lung cancer.** Status: not claimed; the evidence is null on average. Hanna et al. 2020 (BMJ meta-analysis across cancers): lung surgery HR 1.06 per 4 weeks, not significant. A 2025 lung-specific meta-analysis: pooled HR 1.00 (0.99-1.02), I² 97%. Effect probably stage-dependent (sicker-quicker in advanced disease). Neither is in params/sources.yaml. Our pages must not claim survival gains. <https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11357-025-01684-9>
- **`lung-specific-diagnosis-to-treatment`: Finnish lung-specific diagnosis to treatment is 65 to 69 days.** Status: published but not in the registry; conflicts with the 37 d we use. Five Finnish hospitals, 2018 (Acta Oncologica 2025, article 43398). See fi-diagnosis-to-treatment-37d and sim-fi-survives-lung-specific-wait. <https://medicaljournalssweden.se/actaoncologica/article/view/43398>
- **`redesign-studies`: Pathway redesigns elsewhere cut time to treatment (Royal Free 66 to 29 d; Toronto 39.9 to 28.1 d referral to diagnosis; a Norwegian county hospital about 8 and 15 d; a Welsh simulation 10-12 and 20-22 d; English CXR-to-CT 15 to 3 d without changing time to decision).** Status: cited in docs/master_plan.md, not in the registry. Supports the direction of 'order matters'. The master plan itself notes one PubMed link may point to a different paper (England et al.).
- **`capacity-limits`: Some tests are genuinely undersized (London study, Cooke 2022) and fast tracks can lengthen waits for others (Swedish interviews).** Status: cited in docs/master_plan.md and deck backup, not in the registry. The limits of reordering; the simulator reports all patients' waits as a guardrail because of this.
- **`finnish-pet-bronchoscopy-waits`: Finnish waits for PET-CT, bronchoscopy, needle biopsy and the tumour board cycle.** Status: no source found. These steps carry a large share of the simulated Finnish waiting days that rest on assumptions (see the split table and in-fi-waits-half-published). A question for AstraZeneca mentors or a county.
- **`diagnostic-ct-sensitivity`: Diagnostic chest CT sensitivity in symptomatic patients (0.95 used).** Status: no source found; screening LDCT (HOREWEG2014, secondary) and nodule CT (WEIRMCCALL2020, abstract) are proxies. params/test_accuracy.yaml keeps it as an assumption.

## What this adds up to

The research establishes where time is lost in the lung-cancer pathway and which changes have been proposed or tried. What our tool adds is that such changes can be simulated, starting from a Finnish hospital pathway (today a synthetic county) or the Norwegian hospital of Stokstad et al. 2019, and that the changes to the pathway graph that reduce the wait can be searched for and tested on simulated patients. The size of any gain is an estimate that rests on the inputs listed in this register, many of which are our own assumptions.
