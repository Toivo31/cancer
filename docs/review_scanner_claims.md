## 3. 'Where time is lost' (screen 2, flow page card, deck row 3, master plan)

Question from Tuomas: is the "N% of the time to treatment is spent waiting" claim from our simulation, from research, or made up?

Answer shown on the pages: the share is arithmetic on the simulation's output, but each step's wait is an input (booking leads, capacities and assumed extra waits in the scenario file). New `demo/wait_basis.py` tags every waiting day on the critical path of treated cancers (same cohorts and accounting as `stage_breakdown`) with how its input was set, from a mapping written from the scenario's `source:` and `note:` fields (those fields alone do not give a clean per-step tag). An assumed `extra_wait` is always counted as an assumption, separately from the step's booking wait. The exporter writes `wait_basis` for both scenarios; the dashboard (screen 2), the flow page card and the pitch (deck row 3, numbers sheet C9, video script) read it.

Result as exported (Finland, held-out cohorts 301 and 302): waiting is 87% of the critical path; 46% of the waiting days are set from published figures (GP appointment, specialist visit, treatment booking with the flag that its 37-day figure covers all cancers, referral handling) and 54% are our assumption or placeholder (PET-CT, CT, bronchoscopy, needle biopsy, tumour board, X-ray, nurse, paperwork, and the assumed GP re-presentation interval and pre-treatment work-up). Norway: 72% published (STOKSTAD2019 intervals), 28% fitted or assumed.

Difference from the architect's split (52% / 48%): the architect counted the whole GP re-presentation wait and the whole treatment-booking wait as published. Their assumed `extra_wait` parts (re-presentation interval, median 14 d; pre-treatment work-up) are assumptions in the scenario file, so the exporter counts them as such. Also the capacity behind any queue is our input even at a step whose lead is tuned to a published wait; the strip's method note says so.

Headline reworded to "In our model, N% of the time to treatment is waiting for bookings and queues, not the tests", with a visible strip (from our simulation / set from published figures / our assumption or placeholder) and the sentence "This is a consequence of the waits we put in, not a measurement from patient data; it shows where the modelled time sits, and it is only as good as those inputs."
