# Trailer brief: "Waiting is a decision nobody made"

A 2-minute trailer for a hackathon project: an AI decision model and simulator that shortens the wait from first symptoms to cancer treatment. Total length 1:50 to 1:58, never over 2:00. Make it as one film with a narrator, music, and two visual worlds. Everything below is guidance: the second counts are targets, and the attached audio decides the exact cutting.

## Attachments and how to use them

- **Audio (music reference).** Follow its rhythm and structure. About 185 BPM, so a beat every 0.32 s and a bar every 1.3 s. It has a quiet opening, a new layer about 12 seconds after it fades in, a long build, then a big DROP, then full energy, then a dip. Place the film so that it fades in at the start of Part C, the new layer lands as the first dot piles start growing (Part D), and the DROP lands exactly when the piles dissolve (Part G). Shift or trim the music as needed to do this. The music ends with a hard stop at about 1:42, followed by silence.
- **Walt screencaptures.** Reference for the main character's look, clothes and posture only.
- **IBM note image.** A typed note, used in Parts C and I. Use it as the picture whenever the note is on screen.
- **Mood boards.** The video board (waiting rooms, paper, queues in print) is the look of Parts A to C and I. The demo and simulation boards are the look of Parts D to H. Weigh the waiting-room and corridor images most.
- **Animation recording.** Screen recordings of a dot simulation on a black stage. Use this footage directly as the picture for Parts D, F, G and H wherever possible. Where it is not enough, recreate it in the same style.

## Two worlds, one transition

- **The film world (Parts A to C and I):** a 1980s documentary. 16mm grain, slight VHS softness, cold teal-black shadows, grey-teal walls, cream and paper tones, one warm amber accent. Static or very slow camera, shallow focus. Calm and bureaucratic, lightly uneasy.
- **The dot world (Parts D to H):** a pure black stage. Hundreds of small round dots; one dot is one simulated patient. Dots pile up where patients wait, and the piles glow amber the longer they wait. Mint dots are patients treated in time, grey ones treated late, red ones a missed cancer, cyan is the new fast lane. Giant monospaced numerals, tracked monospaced labels, hairline lines, nothing decorative. About 95 percent black.
- **The transition between them is the note.** A typed note is zoomed until its black ink becomes the black stage, and its letters become dots.

Never show computers, monitors, terminals or screens in the film world. No stock-photo smiling doctors, no hospital cliches, no glowing brains. The target of the film is the queue, never the clinicians.

## Narrator

A deadpan documentary narrator, 1980s public-information style: calm, dry, slightly formal, unhurried, never excited. Short sentences with pauses. A hint of irony in the first half, warmer and plainer in the last line. Speak numbers clearly and slowly. Leave silence between lines; the picture does half the talking.

## Scenes

### Part A: The diagnosis (about 12 to 16 s). Near silence.

Original shots, a reinterpretation of a famous cancer-diagnosis scene. No music. Cuts follow the silence and a faint high tinnitus-like tone, not a beat. Muffled low voices only.

1. **The ambulance.** Night, an ambulance light sweeping a wet wall, then the inside ceiling lights passing overhead. No faces. Short.
2. **The appointment.** A plain consultation room. The man from the reference images sits across a desk from a doctor seen from behind or at the edge of frame. His face goes still. Static.
3. **The lips.** Extreme close-up of a doctor's lips speaking, slightly out of focus, the image slowly losing contrast as the sound drops out.
4. **The mustard stain.** Extreme close-up of a white doctor's jacket with a small mustard stain, slow push-in, shallow focus. Mundane, slightly absurd. Hold it one beat longer than is comfortable.
5. **The note.** A doctor's hand, no face, slides a small folded slip of paper across a wooden desk. The man takes it.

### Part B: The waiting room (about 5 to 8 s).

A short drift down a corridor to a door with a sign WAITING ROOM, then a long municipal waiting room seen from the door: rows of plastic chairs, people as quiet silhouettes, tall frosted windows, cold teal light. The man sits. On the far wall, a dot-matrix display in warm amber dots reads "87 DAYS". Slow push-in. A small tag in the corner: "Dramatisation".

- Narrator, while he sits: "Cancer is a word. Waiting is a number."

### Part C: The note is the quote (about 8 to 10 s). The music fades in.

Close on his hands unfolding the slip, then his eyes as he reads. The attached IBM note fills the frame, typed on cream paper: A COMPUTER CAN NEVER BE HELD ACCOUNTABLE / THEREFORE A COMPUTER MUST NEVER MAKE A MANAGEMENT DECISION. Push in until the typed text fills the screen, and keep going into the black of a single letter until the whole frame is black. The push lands on a downbeat at the first strong hit of the build.

- Narrator: "In 1979, a slide at IBM said a computer can never be held accountable. We agree."

### Part D: The wait (about 10 to 12 s). Dot world begins.

From the black, the typed letters come back as small dots and scatter into the first frame of the animation: today's pathway, a lung cancer patient pathway with four labelled stages (DETECTION, REFERRAL, DIAGNOSIS, TREATMENT START). A day counter in the corner ticks up (DAY 000 and onward). Dots move left to right and pile up at the slow steps, and the piles turn amber as they grow. The new music layer arrives as the first pile rises. Three of the biggest piles carry small labels.

- Narrator: "So ours never decides. Each dot is a simulated patient. Where they wait, they pile up. Eighty-seven percent of the days are waiting, not testing."
- On-screen: a caption at the bottom: "Simulated patients".

### Part E: The engine (about 16 to 18 s). Dot world, building.

The piles settle into a flowchart of the pathway (boxes and nodes: nurse or GP, chest X-ray, referral, specialist, CT, biopsy, PET-CT, tumour board, treatment slot). A small clinical note card appears beside it (a synthetic example: "Tired, lost 6 kg. Likely stress. Return if needed.") with the words "lost 6 kg" and "likely stress" highlighted.

Then the decision model, drawn in the same dot-and-hairline style:
1. The note's words and the patient's history flow into one tall bar of small cells. Label: "Everything we know".
2. The allowed next steps appear on the right as cards (for example CT, biopsy, specialist referral, wait and watch).
3. Thin translucent layers between the bar and the cards, with threads from every option crossing every layer: each option sees the others. Label: "Every option sees the others".
4. Bars grow beside the cards as probabilities, and the top option lights cyan. Label: "A probability for every option".
5. A stamp lands on the top option: "A doctor decides".

As the narrator names each job, a label lights in cyan: SECOND OPINION (the note gets a flag: "unexplained weight loss, CT"), AUTOMATION FRAMEWORK (a node changes from a person icon to a machine icon and the dots keep flowing), PATHWAY OPTIMISATION (two versions of the flowchart side by side, one with a shorter route).

- Narrator: "The pathway is a flowchart people set. At each step, a decision model reads the patient's notes and results, compares every option at once, and proposes the next step. A second opinion. A framework for automation. An optimiser for the order."

### Part F: Calibrate and analyse (about 8 to 10 s).

A different pathway: a real Norwegian hospital study. Two lanes, the top one "Observed pathway (Stokstad et al. 2019)". Dots run, amber piles rise, one tall pile at the PET-CT step. A big mint block of treated patients on the right grows to 40%. Two gauges meet with a small click: "Published 40% / Ours 40%". Source caption at the bottom: "Stokstad et al. 2019".

- Narrator: "First, a real Norwegian pathway, rebuilt from the paper: forty percent treated on time, as published. Where does the time go? Into the queues."

### Part G: The drop (about 12 s). Full energy.

On the music's DROP: the piles dissolve and the same dots flow through. The mint block on the bottom lane jumps from 40% to 80%. Small numbers appear beside it: "88% with the faster scanner" and "67% with no new equipment". Then two bars: "More capacity: 40 to 48% (not significant)" and "Better order: 40 to 80%".

- Narrator: "Now the optimiser, told nothing, tries new orders on the same patients. It finds the reorder the authors found. Eighty percent. More capacity barely moves it. Order does."

### Part H: Finland (about 9 to 12 s).

The dots dim behind a clean card in the same typography: "Synthetic Finnish county. Held-out patients. 90-day provisional target." Then big numerals: "18%" then an arrow and "33%", labelled "treated within 90 days, two reorders, no new equipment". Under it: "Vague symptoms 13 to 26%. Alarm symptoms 27 to 50%. Both groups gain." A small footer: "All patients are simulated."

- Narrator: "In a synthetic Finnish county, eighteen percent are treated within ninety days. With two reorders and no new equipment, thirty-three."

### Part I: The ending (about 12 to 16 s). The music stops hard at about 1:42.

Back to the film world, the same wide waiting room as Part B. The amber dots of the wall display lift off and drift away, leaving the wall blank. The man unfolds the note again: the typed text has changed to THEREFORE A HUMAN DECIDES / WITH THE WHOLE SYSTEM IN VIEW. A side door opens; a doctor, seen from behind with no face, holds it open and says nothing. The man stands. Cut to black, one full second. Then an end card on black: "Waiting is a decision nobody made." and smaller below, "A doctor decides every case. All patients are simulated." A single low note and a few typewriter clicks are the only sound.

- Narrator: "A doctor decides every case. Waiting is a decision nobody made. Now someone can."

## Rhythm rules

- Parts A and B live on the silence. Cut when the image asks to be cut, not on a beat.
- From the music fade-in at Part C, every cut and every text or number change lands on a beat. Shot lengths should be whole bars or whole beats of the audio, and you decide the exact lengths. Cut a little tighter as the music builds.
- The narrator speaks in the gaps and over the ducked music (music about 12 dB lower under the voice). No line should be rushed to fit; shorten a picture instead.
- Never let any scene run past its guide length by more than a couple of seconds. If the total runs over 2:00, shorten Parts A, B and H first.

## Text rules

- Render on-screen text exactly as written, in a monospaced or typewriter face, light on dark. If text cannot be rendered reliably, leave a clean empty space for it and do not invent letters; it will be added in post.
- On-screen text list: WAITING ROOM; 87 DAYS; Dramatisation; Simulated patients; DETECTION, REFERRAL, DIAGNOSIS, TREATMENT START; DAY counter; the note texts above; Everything we know; Every option sees the others; A probability for every option; A doctor decides; SECOND OPINION; AUTOMATION FRAMEWORK; PATHWAY OPTIMISATION; Stokstad et al. 2019; Published 40% / Ours 40%; 80%, 88%, 67%; More capacity: 40 to 48% (not significant); Better order: 40 to 80%; 18%, 33%, 13 to 26%, 27 to 50%; Waiting is a decision nobody made.

## Consistency

Same man, same clothes and same grade in every film-world shot. If his face cannot hold across shots, show him from behind, in profile or in shadow. The dot world uses one palette only: black, off-white, amber, mint, pink, cyan, red and grey, with one meaning each.
