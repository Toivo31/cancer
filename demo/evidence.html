This is a plausibility check on one hospital, not a forecast for patients

  • One hospital, … patients with early-stage lung cancer, specialist care only. It says nothing about primary care, where most Finnish waiting sits.
  • The paper's scanner and reordering figures are the authors' own re-timing of recorded waits, not results of a redesign that was carried out. We compare with estimates, not outcomes.
  • Every patient in our model is synthetic. Five quantities the paper does not report were fitted, and … settings of them fit today's pathway equally well. The bars show that spread.
  • Our range and the paper's interval measure different things: theirs comes from a small cohort, ours from the unknown inputs. Any single number from the simulator carries both.
  • The optimiser ran once, at the best fit, from today's pathway, with no new equipment; its pathway was then evaluated unchanged in every setting. We report the faster scanner and the reordering separately, as the authors did, and never combine them.
  • It did not improve on the authors' redesign, and it missed the part of it that reduces sampling procedures. Its fourth change (tissue sampling planned at referral triage) is not in the paper and needs clinical review.
  • "Reordering beats buying the scanner" holds in … of … settings, not in all of them.
  • We worked from summaries of the paper's text and figure captions; the figures themselves were not inspected.
  • Inputs of the Norwegian scenario: … blocks, … cite the paper or another source and … are our assumptions.
  • Nothing here has been reviewed by a clinician, and nothing is validated on real patients.
